What I'm working on

Some of the things I'm working on with AI in documentation.

01
Feedback + remediation

Self-healing documentation.

I'm building a system that brings together documentation feedback and signals from multiple sources, normalizes the data, applies a consistent impact-based priority, and routes work against the appropriate SLA.

The next step is automated remediation with a human approval checkpoint.

02
Product source

Documentation closer to the product.

AI gives documentation a much more direct relationship with the product itself. Instead of relying primarily on individual SMEs, meetings, Slack, and other sources that can quickly become incomplete or stale, I'm working to connect documentation directly to product repositories.

I'm currently bringing together repositories across product teams so we can use them to identify documentation gaps, validate docs against the product, track feature availability and development, and follow features through GA and release. It improves accuracy while reducing our dependence on individual SMEs.

One example: writers on my team are already working directly in product source. Automation extracts UI strings for translation, and docs can raise merge requests to check the localized strings back into the codebase.
03
Quality + governance

Automated content governance.

I've used automated checkpoints in content governance for years— for example, preventing work from closing until required release and quality criteria are met.

I'm now extending that approach with AI-driven validation and rules that can be applied consistently across content at scale.

What I've done

Preparing the documentation for where the product was going.

Content rearchitecture

2-year effort
16 writers
9 doc sets
41 books

As our product strategy moved away from individual products toward a more unified platform, the existing documentation structure was becoming a problem. Customers had to understand our products, versions, and management interfaces just to find the right content.

I led a two-year effort to restructure the experience around what customers were actually trying to accomplish: consolidating fragmented documentation, connecting workflows across products, and creating a common information architecture that could work across search, product, support, and the docs site.

We started the work ahead of the product transition, so the documentation architecture was ready for where the product was going rather than catching up afterward. The work was part of the case for my promotion to Director in 2023.
